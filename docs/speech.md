# Speech: A Universe in a Browser Tab (3 minutes, ~400 words)

**[Opening — question hook]**

Hello, everyone. Before I start, let me ask you a question: what happens when a star gets too close to a black hole? Most people think it just disappears. The truth is much cooler — the star gets stretched like spaghetti, heated up, and swallowed. In my project, you can watch that happen with your own eyes.

**[What it is]**

I built a Planet Physics Simulator. It is a small universe that lives inside a browser tab. You can create stars and planets, throw them with your mouse, and watch them crash, bounce, or merge. Everything pulls on everything else, following the same law of gravity that keeps the Earth around the Sun.

**[The black hole — the shine part]**

My favorite part is the black hole. It is not just a dark circle — it uses a special formula that scientists invented, because real black holes pull much harder than normal gravity. There is a line around it called the event horizon: cross that line, and there is no way back. And when you fly a spaceship near it, the screen shows something unbelievable — time itself slows down for you. That is Einstein's theory, alive on your screen.

**[Stars live and die]**

Stars in my simulator are alive, too. A small star burns quietly for a long time. A big star swallows everything around it, grows and grows — and then, BOOM! A supernova. It collapses into a black hole, and the show starts again. You can speed up time by a thousand times and watch the whole story of a star in less than a minute.

**[Playing with friends]**

You don't have to explore alone. The physics runs on a server, so all players share the same universe at the same time. Like Minecraft, you can open your universe to your friends with a room code — and as the host, your world is the one everyone lives in.

**[Closing]**

Building it taught me that gravity is not just words in a textbook — it is something you can play with. So the next time you look at the night sky, remember: the universe follows rules, and rules can be understood. Maybe one of us will understand them first.

Thank you!

---

*Delivery tips: pause after the opening question; slow down at "stretched like spaghetti" and "BOOM"; the last two sentences are the punch — look at the audience, speak slowly. ≈ 400 words ≈ 3 minutes.*
